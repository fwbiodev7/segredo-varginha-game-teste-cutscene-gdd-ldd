## Arquitetura

Arquivo final: `Assets/Resources/Varginha/WorldArtV2/Architecture.png`.

Use case: style-transfer. Asset: production 2D RPG architecture texture atlas for a remaster of the Brazilian Industrial school game. Reference 1 SchoolComputerLab gives the actual original game's beige/blue/dark-metal color language and clean orthographic 3/4 top-down pixel rendering. Reference 2 FurnitureV1 gives the original chunky hand-painted pixel style, dark outlines, warm woods. Create a NEW 1024x1024 square opaque atlas exactly 4 columns x 4 rows of 256x256 tiles, no gutters, no labels. Each cell is a fully filled square seamless texture, no perspective and no objects. Row1: warm brown horizontal wood floor planks; pale cream school ceramic floor with very fine subtle grid lines; muted gray church stone tiles; calm green grass. Row2: compact warm brown dirt; smooth dark navy gray asphalt (NO cracks); calm teal water with small sparse ripples; muted blue gray laboratory ceramic floor. Row3: cream interior wall with dark wooden skirting along its bottom; beige school plaster wall with brick skirting at bottom; gray church wall with simple stone skirting; old gray stone ruin wall. Row4: cream laboratory wall with desaturated teal skirting; garage concrete wall with navy skirting; smooth light gray sidewalk pavers with faint joints; brown wooden bridge decking. Coherent crisp pixel art at logical 32x32 per cell enlarged nearest neighbor 8x, restrained color ramps (4-6 colors per material), large readable clusters, clean borders, low-frequency texture, no noisy dithering, no shiny realistic materials, no gradients, no shadows crossing cells, no bevel embossing, no heavy high-contrast floor grids. This atlas becomes walkable floors and upright walls underneath the original furniture; readability of sprites is more important than texture detail.

## Móveis

Arquivo final: `Assets/Resources/Varginha/WorldArtV2/Furniture.png`.

Use case: style-transfer. Production transparent 2D game furniture sprite atlas, not a scene. Remaster the original Brazilian mystery game furniture shown in reference FurnitureV1 and SchoolComputerLab, keeping its straight-on 3/4 overhead RPG angle, dark crisp outlines, warm brown wood, cream ceramics, blue upholstery, dark metal frames. EXACTLY 6 columns x4 rows equally sized cells on a 1536x1024 TRANSPARENT image. One isolated sprite centered in each 256x256 cell with AT LEAST 24 pixels transparent margin on every side; no sprite crosses its cell. No captions, no numbers, no decoration outside objects, no backdrop. Row1: wood writing desk with drawers; blue upholstered wooden chair; blue two-seat sofa; rectangular low wood coffee table; wood bookcase with colorful books; small bedside drawer. Row2: tall three-drawer wood dresser; kitchen sink cabinet; single bed with cream pillow and blue blanket; beige computer desk with black CRT monitor and dark tower matching reference; long simple church pew; small church stone altar with red cloth. Row3: old CRT television on short wooden cabinet; cream gas stove; cream two-door refrigerator; slender black street lamp; cork notice board; closed brown wood door. Row4: lush rounded green tree with trunk; rounded low green shrub; dark metal science bench with a few glass samples; blue VW Beetle car from overhead (reference stylistic only, unused in game); wood workbench with a few tools; dark metal tool rack. Sharp tasteful hand-painted PIXEL ART, 32-64 logical pixel width per sprite visibly enlarged with nearest-neighbor, compact readable colored clusters, 4-6 colors per material, strong silhouettes. Match original references, no photorealistic texture, no isometric skew, no 3D render, no smoothing, no ghost duplicate objects, no cast shadow outside the sprite, no black background. Fully transparent negative space.
